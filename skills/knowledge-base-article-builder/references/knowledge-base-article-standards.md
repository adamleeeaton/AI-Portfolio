# Knowledge-Base Article Standards

These neutral standards help keep organizational knowledge-base content consistent, searchable, maintainable, and placed at the correct level. Replace bracketed values with the target organization's approved conventions.

## Core principles

- Use the exact naming patterns defined by [organization naming standard].
- Keep terminology and titles consistent across related pages.
- Place information at the narrowest appropriate level.
- Separate general information from team-, department-, program-, or process-specific information.
- Separate procedures, requirements, FAQs, reference information, and next steps when that improves findability.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication.
- Preserve source accuracy and identify content that needs review.

## Suggested hierarchy

Use only the levels supported by the target organization. A common pattern is:

1. Organization or service area
2. Department, team, or product area
3. Program, product, or audience
4. Specialization, option, or sub-process
5. Individual process, reference article, or FAQ

The correct level depends on who owns the content, who uses it, how often it changes, and whether the information applies broadly or narrowly.

## Naming conventions

Use the organization's official names, abbreviations, identifiers, and capitalization. If no standard exists, prefer:

- `[Area] Information`
- `[Area] Process`
- `[Area] Requirements`
- `[Area] Frequently Asked Questions`
- `[Program or Product] [Topic]`

Do not expose internal codes, system names, or organizational labels in a public portfolio example unless they are intentionally public and approved for publication.

## Article organization

Use the sequence that fits the source:

1. Title
2. Brief overview or purpose
3. Requirements, eligibility, or important context
4. Numbered procedure, when applicable
5. Important notes, restrictions, exceptions, or limitations
6. Common Questions, when useful and supported
7. Next steps, contact, or escalation information
8. Source or review note, when requested

Use only relevant sections. Do not add content merely to fill a template.

## Common Questions standards

Include Common Questions when the source supports reliable answers about:

- Audience or eligibility
- Required steps
- Timing or deadlines
- Prerequisites
- Restrictions or exceptions
- Available choices
- What happens after completion
- Where to get help

Do not include a question when the answer is unsupported, speculative, duplicative, or too trivial to clarify the article.

## Quality-control checklist

Before publishing or updating an article, confirm that:

- The article is placed at the correct organizational level.
- The title follows the approved naming format.
- Official terminology, identifiers, and links are accurate.
- The article is grounded in supplied or approved source material.
- Procedures appear in the correct order.
- Restrictions, exceptions, warnings, and prerequisites are retained.
- FAQs are useful, supported, and not duplicative.
- The content is readable, searchable, accessible, and easy to maintain.
- Local privacy, safety, referral, escalation, and approval requirements are included where applicable.
- No private, confidential, or personally identifying information remains in the published or portfolio version.
